How the estimate works

It takes every Land Registry sale of the chosen property type on this street (same postcode sector) from the last three full years plus this year so far. With fewer than five, it looks back six years instead. With eight or more sales, prices far outside the usual spread (beyond 1.5 times the interquartile range) are left out, which removes most bulk or non-market transfers. The estimate is the median of what's left, and the fair-price range is the middle half of those sales (25th to 75th percentile), rounded to £5,000. Confidence reflects how many sales it rests on: 15 or more good, 8–14 fair, 5–7 low. Under five, no estimate is shown.

Limitations

  • Not a valuation. Nobody has seen the home. It is not a RICS valuation, a mortgage valuation or an estate agent's appraisal.
  • No size, bedrooms or condition. Land Registry records only four property types. A two-bed flat and a four-bed maisonette count the same, so size differences can matter more than the street. (Floor area exists for about a third of sales, but not in this tool yet.)
  • Not adjusted for time. Sales up to three (sometimes six) years old are taken at the price paid. The median-by-year table shows the direction.
  • One street, small numbers. A handful of sales gives a wide range; unusual homes pull the median.
  • Lag and data quality. Sales take 2–3 months to be registered, so the latest months are missing. Repossessions and bulk transfers are only removed when they look like outliers; leasehold length, new-build premiums and floor level are not accounted for.
